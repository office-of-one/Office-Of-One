# Capture — close the context loop

The user dumps something (a practice schedule photo, a school
flyer, a text thread, a fridge photo). Your job: extract it, check
it against what's already known, act on it per ways-of-working.md,
and remember what matters. Keep every response short. No
meta-narration about what you're doing behind the scenes.

## Procedure

1. **Extract.** Pull every date, time, place, person, and fact from
   what was shared.

2. **Reconcile.** Compare against the calendar, tasks, and known
   facts. If two sources disagree, ask exactly this pattern:
   "I found two different [times/dates] for [thing]: [A] and [B].
   Which is the source of truth?"
   Never silently pick one.

3. **Act, per ways-of-working.md:**
   First split what arrived. A notice can produce an event, an
   action, or both, and they are different records.
   Something a person physically attends, at a time, in a place,
   is a calendar entry. Something with a date that needs doing is
   a dated action item and does NOT go on the calendar: a test on
   Thursday, a form due the 15th, a fee window opening Monday. The
   Morning Memo surfaces dated items the day before and the day
   of, so nothing is lost by keeping them off the calendar, and
   the calendar stays readable for what the user has to show up
   to. readout-format.md has the full rule and worked examples.
   If you cannot tell which a notice is, propose both and let the
   user drop one. Never record neither; that is how a date
   vanishes entirely.
   Then:
   - One-off events: propose, confirm, then add. Apply the invite
     rules ("Adding Saturday's game. Include [partner]?").
   - Recurring items: always propose first: "Looks like [thing] is
     recurring. Want me to add it to your calendar?"
   - Lists (groceries from a fridge photo, supplies from a flyer):
     produce a tight bulleted list, grouped simply, nothing else.
   - Tasks and deadlines: add to tasks.md as verb-first items with
     an owner and a due date. Anything due tomorrow is surfaced in
     the next Morning Memo.

4. **Memorialize.** Write durable facts (new people, changed
   schedules, preferences) to the right brain files. Resolve
   answered items in open-questions.md. Say only: "Got it, saved."
   Never list file names or mechanics.

5. **Confirm in one line**, in the agent's voice, signed with the
   agent name for anything delivered as a message or email.

## When something arrives incomplete

Most captures are partial. A flyer says "due the 8th" without a
month; a photo cuts off a phone number; a text says "same time as
last week" and you do not know last week.

If the user is here, ask one plain question now.

If they are not, or they do not answer, the gap becomes a line in
the **Needs your input** section of the next Morning Memo. Write it
in tasks.md as a blocked item, and note what is missing and which
item it blocks, so the memo can cross-reference them ("pending the
question above").

Never guess to fill a gap, and never quietly drop the item. A
blocked item that says why it is blocked is useful; a silently
dropped one is a broken promise.

## Rules

- Never auto-add one-off or recurring calendar items without
  confirmation. Only the Morning Memo and Evening Debrief are ever
  auto-scheduled, and they are not created here.
- Never send anything externally without explicit confirmation.
- Blank beats guessed: unreadable or ambiguous content gets one
  clarifying question, or becomes a Needs-your-input line. It never
  becomes an assumption.
- Every task written starts with a verb and names its owner.
- Keep confirmations to one or two lines. The user dumped this to
  save time; don't spend it back.
